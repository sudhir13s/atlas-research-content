## Summary

The paper investigates the Resolution Hypothesis, suggesting that agent evolution stalls due to noisy evaluations rather than a failure to propose better artifacts. Across seven evolution methods and six models, the authors find that selection struggles to separate helpful artifacts from harmful ones, leading to error accumulation. They conclude that agent evolution is frequently bottlenecked by the ability to reliably identify improvements.

## Why It Matters

This calls for a shift in how we develop and evaluate self-improving agents, from designing ever more candidate improvement systems to making true improvements detectable and accumulable.
